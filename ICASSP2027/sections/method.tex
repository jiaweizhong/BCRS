\section{Proposed Method}
\label{sec:method}

The overall architecture of HESOD is illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}(a). Given an input image $\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$, a shallow stem extracts dense feature map $\mathbf{F} \in \mathbb{R}^{H_s \times W_s \times C}$. The \emph{Dual-Evidence Spatial Selector} then proceeds in two steps: (i) a priority head fuses semantic objectness with channel-pooled spectral saliency (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}(b)) to generate continuous priority map $\mathbf{S} \in [0, 1]^{H_s \times W_s}$; and (ii) an AdaSlicer module dynamically crops candidate foreground patches $\mathcal{P}$ directly from $\mathbf{F}$. Downstream backbone, neck, and lightweight ISPP decoupled heads evaluate only these retained patches, supervised by an object-level soft-coverage loss and scale-aware regression (\S\ref{ssec:coverage}, \S\ref{ssec:head}).

\subsection{Dual-Evidence Priority Estimation}
\label{ssec:evidence}

To rescue targets with collapsed semantic responses, HESOD couples semantic confidence with structural gradient saliency (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}(b)).

Let $\mathbf{f}_i \in \mathbb{R}^C$ denote the feature vector at spatial location $i$ in $\mathbf{F}$. The semantic branch predicts class logits $\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{sem}}_i = \mathrm{Conv}_{1\times 1}(\mathbf{f}_i) \in \mathbb{R}^{N_c}$. In parallel, the spectral branch extracts high-frequency structural gradients by compressing channels via max- and mean-pooling:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{pooled}} = \left[ \max_c \mathbf{F}_{:,:,c} \,\|\, \frac{1}{C}\sum_{c=1}^C \mathbf{F}_{:,:,c} \right] \in \mathbb{R}^{H_s \times W_s \times 2},
\label{eq:channel_pool}
\end{equation}
where $[\cdot \,\|\, \cdot]$ denotes concatenation. Channel compression ensures spatial filtering complexity remains $\mathcal{O}(1)$ regardless of backbone width $C$. Learnable depthwise $3\times 3$ convolutions (initialized via Laplacian and Sobel filters) then filter $\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{pooled}}$, followed by a $1\times 1$ projection to yield spectral logits $\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{spec}}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{N_c}$.

To prevent confidence dilution on weak targets, evidence fusion $\mathcal{F}$ satisfies \emph{evidence preservation} $\mathcal{F}(p_s, p_f) \ge \max(p_s, p_f)$, producing priority score $s_i \in [0, 1]$:
\begin{equation}
s_i = \begin{cases}
\max\!\left(\sigma(\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{sem}}_i),\, \sigma(\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{spec}}_i)\right), & \text{Max (cluttered)} \\
\sigma\!\left(\mathrm{Conv}_{1\times 1}\!\left(\left[ \mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{sem}}_i \,\|\, \mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{spec}}_i \right]\right)\right), & \text{Concat (sparse)}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}
where Max preserves salient cues in dense clutter (UAVDT), while Concat provides smooth blending in sparse scenes (SeaPerson). Candidate cells with $s_i > \tau$ are clustered into bounding patches $\mathcal{P}$.

\subsection{Object-Level Soft-Coverage Loss}
\label{ssec:coverage}

Standard pixel-wise binary cross-entropy (BCE) evaluates grid cells independently, failing to guarantee instance coverage: a selector may over-allocate cells to large objects while dropping tiny objects whose receptive fields contain only weak responses. To prevent premature false negatives, we formulate a differentiable object-level soft-coverage loss.

Let $\mathcal{N}(j)$ denote the set of selector cells overlapping ground-truth box $j \in \{1, \dots, N_{\mathrm{gt}}\}$. Assuming independent cell activations, the probability that instance $j$ is covered by at least one selected cell is:
\begin{equation}
p_j^{\mathrm{cover}} = 1 - \prod_{i \in \mathcal{N}(j)} (1 - s_i).
\label{eq:cover_prob}
\end{equation}
The soft-coverage loss directly optimizes joint instance coverage:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cover}} = -\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{gt}}} \sum_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{gt}}} w_j \log\left( p_j^{\mathrm{cover}} + \epsilon \right),
\label{eq:cover_loss}
\end{equation}
where $w_j = \operatorname{clip}(4/a_j, 1, 5)$ penalizes misses on micro targets ($a_j < 4$) based on box area $a_j$. The total training objective is $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{det}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{sel}}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BCE}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{cov}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cover}})$, with $\lambda_{\mathrm{sel}}=0.2$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{cov}}=0.5$.

\subsection{Efficient Decoupled Head and Scale-Aware Loss}
\label{ssec:head}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\columnwidth]{figures/ISPPHead.pdf}
\vspace{-2mm}
\caption{Inverted Residual Decoupled Head (ISPP).}
\label{fig:ispphead}
\end{figure}

To reduce computational overhead while boosting localization precision, we incorporate an inverted residual partial-convolution (ISPP) decoupled head~\cite{ESODYOLO} and a Scale-Aware Balancing Loss (SABL)~\cite{SSABNet}.

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:ispphead}, the ISPP head expands neck features to $2C_1$, partitioning them into an active branch ($0.25$ ratio) processed by $3\times 3$ Partial Convolution (PConv) and an identity branch ($0.75$ ratio), where $\textcircled{\scriptsize C}$ denotes channel concatenation. After linear projection and residual addition, decoupled $1\times 1$ heads predict classification, box regression, and objectness independently, reducing compute while eliminating task interference.

Furthermore, standard IoU-based regression suffers from vanishing gradients on micro objects due to extreme sensitivity to tiny spatial misalignments. To ensure robust localization, SABL dynamically balances Gaussian Wasserstein distance ($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NWD}} = 1 - e^{-D_W / 12}$) and Complete IoU (CIoU) via scale-dependent gating $\mu(s) = \exp(-(s/32)^6)$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{box}} = 1 - \mathrm{IoU} + \mu(s)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NWD}} + (1 - \mu(s))(\ell_{\mathrm{ctr}} + \alpha v),
\label{eq:sabl_loss}
\end{equation}
where $s = \sqrt{w \cdot h}$ is the bounding box geometric scale, $\ell_{\mathrm{ctr}}$ penalizes center distance, and $v$ measures aspect consistency. For micro targets ($s \ll 32\text{ px}$), $\mu(s) \to 1$, activating Wasserstein distance to provide non-vanishing gradients under zero IoU overlap; for larger objects ($s \gg 32\text{ px}$), $\mu(s) \to 0$, smoothly reverting to standard CIoU regression.
