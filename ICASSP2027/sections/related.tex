\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Small Object Detection in Aerial Imagery}
\label{ssec:related_sod}

Detecting micro targets in high-resolution aerial and maritime vision is challenged by extreme scale disparity, low contrast, and massive background areas~\cite{VisDrone,UAVDT,TinyPerson,SeaDronesSee}. Classical solutions employ density-guided patch cropping (e.g., ClusDet~\cite{ClusDet}, DMNet~\cite{DMNet}, UFPMP-Det~\cite{UFPMP}) or multi-stage coarse-to-fine slicing (e.g., ResBiDet~\cite{ResBiDet}). While focusing compute, these approaches incur redundant feature extraction across overlapping tiles and complex boundary stitching. Other works design specialized label assignment and regression metrics, such as Normalized Gaussian Wasserstein Distance (NWD)~\cite{NWD}, RFLA~\cite{RFLA}, and scale-aware balancing (SSABNet)~\cite{SSABNet}. However, dense detectors uniformly evaluate all spatial locations, leaving background arithmetic redundancy unresolved.

\subsection{Selective and Dynamic Computation}
\label{ssec:related_selective}

Dynamic computation reduces arithmetic overhead by evaluating only prospective foreground regions. AutoFocus~\cite{AutoFocus} predicts focus pixels for multi-scale inference; QueryDet~\cite{QueryDet} accelerates feature pyramids through cascaded sparse queries; and CEASC~\cite{CEASC} designs adaptive activation masks. ESOD~\cite{ESOD} introduced an early-stage spatial selector after the stem to route foreground patches (AdaSlicer) to downstream stages. Nonetheless, existing selectors depend strictly on single semantic activations and independent per-cell BCE loss, leaving them vulnerable to early-stage pruning. In contrast, HESOD operates in a unified feature-space selective pipeline, combining dual-evidence routing with joint soft-coverage optimization.

\subsection{Spectral Saliency and Frequency Representations}
\label{ssec:related_spectral}

Frequency-domain cues provide rich spatial structural priors. SET~\cite{SET} demonstrated that micro objects preserve distinct high-frequency gradients even when semantic responses fade, though it applies dense frequency operations across feature pyramids. HESOD instead repurposes spectral saliency as an ultra-lightweight, channel-pooled routing signal to ensure recall-safe selection at negligible overhead.
