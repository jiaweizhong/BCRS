\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}


\subsection{Experiment Settings}
\label{ssec:settings}

We evaluate HESOD on two demanding high-resolution small-object benchmarks: \textbf{UAVDT}~\cite{UAVDT} ($1280 \times 1280\text{ px}$, urban vehicle surveillance across 3 classes) and \textbf{SeaPerson}~\cite{TinyPerson,SeaDronesSee} ($2048 \times 2048\text{ px}$, maritime search-and-rescue with $>85\%$ micro targets $<32\text{ px}$). Both benchmarks evaluate full-resolution images without artificial pre-cropping.

All models employ a YOLOv5m detector in PyTorch and train with SGD for 50 epochs under selective protocols~\cite{ESOD}. Detection accuracy is assessed via COCO $\mathrm{mAP@.5}$ and scale-stratified recall across bins: \emph{Very Tiny} ($<16^2$), \emph{Tiny} ($16^2\text{--}32^2$), and \emph{Small} ($32^2\text{--}96^2$). Model efficiency is measured by parameters, GFLOPs, FPS, and Bounding Patch Recall (BPR).

\textbf{Comparison with Dense Baselines.} As shown in Table~\ref{tab:main_results}, classical dense detectors incur prohibitive arithmetic overhead on multi-megapixel vision grids due to uniform convolution. Compared to Faster R-CNN and RetinaNet on SeaPerson ($2048\times 2048$), HESOD delivers substantial accuracy gains of \textbf{+22.3\,pp} and \textbf{+30.1\,pp} $\mathrm{mAP@.5}$, while slashing GFLOPs by \textbf{7.4$\times$} and \textbf{4.5$\times$} at \textbf{80.7 FPS}, confirming that selective computation is essential for edge deployment.

\textbf{Comparison with Selective Baseline (ESOD).} Against ESOD, HESOD achieves clear accuracy, recall, and parameter advantages across both benchmarks. On UAVDT ($1280\times 1280$), HESOD boosts $\mathrm{mAP@.5}$ by \textbf{+0.7\,pp} (0.392 vs. 0.385) and overall instance recovery by \textbf{+3.13\,pp} (88.30\% vs. 85.17\%, recovering +11,700 targets) with consistent recall gains across all vehicle classes (\emph{car}: +2.88\,pp, \emph{truck}: +4.82\,pp, \emph{bus}: +13.78\,pp) and BPR reaching \textbf{0.940} (+5.6\,pp) at 102.9 FPS. On SeaPerson ($2048\times 2048$), HESOD advances $\mathrm{mAP@.5}$ by \textbf{+2.4\,pp} (0.774 vs. 0.750) and BPR by \textbf{+4.4\,pp} (0.991 vs. 0.947), while cutting parameters by \textbf{27.6\%} (25.9M vs. 35.8M) at 80.7 FPS, establishing a superior accuracy-efficiency Pareto frontier.

\textbf{Evidence Preservation and Adaptive Fusion.} Table~\ref{tab:ablation} isolates module contributions and reveals that optimal routing fusion is domain-adaptive. On UAVDT, affine linear concatenation (Arm 5, 0.371 $\mathrm{mAP}$) suffers from confidence dilution when one branch is faint, violating evidence preservation; switching to Elementwise Max (Arm 6) strictly preserves high-confidence evidence, boosting $\mathrm{mAP@.5}$ to \textbf{0.395} (+2.4\,pp) and Total Recall to \textbf{90.36\%}. Conversely, on ultra-sparse SeaPerson, Dual-Concat (Arm 5, \textbf{0.772}) outperforms Dual-Max (Arm 6, 0.766), showing that smooth feature blending excels in isolated maritime scenes. Channel-pooled spectral routing (Arm 4) preserves unpooled accuracy (Arm 3) at $\mathcal{O}(1)$ spatial complexity across both benchmarks.

\textbf{Co-Design of Head and Loss via Staged Training.} Adopting the inverted residual ISPP decoupled head cuts parameters by \textbf{27.6\%} (25.9M vs. 35.8M) and slashes GFLOPs (74.9 on UAVDT, 209.0 on SeaPerson). While joint end-to-end training of downstream heads can perturb shallow selector representations, staged training cleanly decouples routing from fine-grained regression, preserving peak 0.392 $\mathrm{mAP}$ and maintaining 102.9 FPS on UAVDT. Combining scale-aware Wasserstein regression (SABL) further enhances sub-16px localization, reaching peak \emph{Very Tiny} recall of \textbf{77.14\%} on SeaPerson.

\textbf{Error Bottleneck Diagnosis.} Stratified analysis confirms that target recovery is strongest on \emph{Tiny} (+4.81\,pp) and \emph{Very Tiny} (+3.11\,pp) strata. Error diagnosis reveals that dual routing and coverage optimization reduce selector-dropped false negatives from $25.6\%$ in baseline ESOD to \textbf{$19.2\%$}, shifting the remaining bottleneck to downstream refinement.

\textbf{Qualitative Comparison.} As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser} on UAVDT and SeaPerson, baseline ESOD misses weak-contrast micro targets due to semantic degradation, whereas HESOD's dual-evidence routing and scale-aware regression accurately localize all tiny instances without false alarms.
