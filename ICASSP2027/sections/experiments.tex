\section{EXPERIMENTS}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

We evaluate HESOD on two demanding high-resolution small-object benchmarks: \textbf{UAVDT}~\cite{UAVDT} ($1280 \times 1280$ px, urban vehicle surveillance across 3 classes, 19.9\% extremely tiny targets $< 16^2$ px) and \textbf{SeaPerson}~\cite{SeaDronesSee, TinyPerson} ($2048 \times 2048$ px, maritime search-and-rescue with $>85\%$ targets $< 32$ px). Both benchmarks use full-resolution images without artificial cropping.

Selective detectors (ESOD family and our ablations) employ YOLOv5m backbones in PyTorch 2.8 trained for 50 epochs under selective protocols~\cite{ESOD}. Dense and query baselines (Faster R-CNN, RetinaNet, QueryDet) retain canonical ResNet-50-FPN backbones, evaluated on identical test splits and resolutions. All models are benchmarked on an NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU. Metrics include COCO $\mathrm{mAP@.5}$ and $\mathrm{mAP@.5:.95}$, scale-stratified recall across four bins (Very Tiny $< 16^2$, Tiny $16^2\text{--}32^2$, Small $32^2\text{--}96^2$, Med/Large $> 96^2$ px), and, following ESOD~\cite{ESOD}, Best Possible Recall for objects' bounding boxes ($\mathrm{BPR}^{\mathrm{box}}$), measuring the fraction of ground-truth targets covered ($>50\%$ overlap) by selected patches.

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:main_results}, traditional dense detectors suffer from severe efficiency bottlenecks on high-resolution images: Faster R-CNN only achieves 18.8/23.1 FPS on UAVDT/SeaPerson with GFLOPs as high as 772.5/1546.8; QueryDet~\cite{QueryDet} is limited by its dynamic query mechanism, with total recall dropping to only 41.89\% on UAVDT.
HESOD outperforms all baselines: on UAVDT, mAP@.5 is 5.8, 9.8, and 13.7 pp higher than Faster R-CNN, RetinaNet, and QueryDet respectively at 5.0$\times$--5.6$\times$ higher FPS (106.1 vs.~18.8--21.1); on SeaPerson, mAP@.5 exceeds all baselines by 14.6$\sim$30.0 pp at 2.9$\times$--5.8$\times$ higher FPS, with Very Tiny recall reaching 75.71\% (+27.0 pp over QueryDet).

Against the same-architecture selective baseline ESOD~\cite{ESOD}: on UAVDT, mAP@.5 increases from 0.385 to 0.394 (+0.9 pp), BPR from 0.884 to 0.940 (+5.6 pp), total recall by +3.66 pp, with bus class recall jumping from 67.7\% to 81.4\% (+13.7 pp); parameters are reduced from 35.9M to 26.0M ($-$27.5\%) at 106.1 FPS.
On SeaPerson, mAP@.5 increases from 0.750 to 0.773 (+2.3 pp), BPR reaches 0.991 (+4.4 pp), total recall increases by +3.33 pp, successfully recalling more than 10,000 micro maritime targets missed by ESOD; GFLOPs only increase by 3.0\%, parameters are compressed by 27.6\% at 80.6 FPS.
The modest GFLOPs overhead stems from the selector routing more genuine target regions downstream rather than computational redundancy; HESOD controls this within 10\% via the lightweight head design while maintaining high patch coverage (94.0\%/99.1\% BPR on UAVDT/SeaPerson).

\subsection{Ablation Studies and Scale Analysis}

We conduct controlled ablation experiments on the ESOD baseline by adding components incrementally, with results in Table~\ref{tab:ablation_and_recall}.

\textbf{Dual-Evidence Fusion.} Using only the semantic branch yields 0.906 BPR and 84.82\% total recall on UAVDT, showing limited improvement due to semantic feature collapse during downsampling; the spectral-only branch achieves stronger BPR of 0.942 and 88.87\% total recall, as gradient features remain robust on micro targets.
Comparing fusion strategies, linear concatenation (Arm 5) achieves only 0.371 mAP@.5---lower than either single branch---because weak semantic responses introduce destructive interference that suppresses confident spectral activations. In contrast, element-wise maximum (Dual-Max, Arm 6) implements an evidence-preserving OR gate, achieving 0.395/0.778 mAP@.5 and 0.940/0.991 BPR on UAVDT/SeaPerson, the best among all single-stage selector configurations.

\textbf{Soft-Coverage Loss.} The ESOD baseline trained with per-pixel BCE achieves only 0.884 BPR and 79.43\% Very Tiny recall on UAVDT. With the proposed $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cover}}$, the semantic branch's Very Tiny recall is flat at 79.13\%, while the spectral branch's rises to 82.43\%; under Dual-Max fusion it reaches 85.69\% (+6.26 pp). The noisy-OR formulation provides strong gradients to low-scoring covering cells when a target is under-covered, encouraging at least one cell to exceed the routing threshold and reducing unrecoverable missed detections.

\textbf{ISPP Head Efficiency.} Without the ISPP head, Dual-Max incurs 90.1 GFLOPs and 102.3 FPS on UAVDT. Adding ISPP reduces GFLOPs by 16.9\% to 74.9, boosts FPS to 106.1, and compresses parameters by 27.5\%, with mAP@.5 decreasing by only 0.001 and BPR unchanged at 0.940. On SeaPerson, ISPP cuts GFLOPs by 18.2\% and parameters by 27.6\%, lifting FPS from 73.8 to 80.6 with negligible accuracy loss (0.778 $\to$ 0.773 mAP).

\textbf{Channel Pooling.} Without channel pooling, full-width spectral filtering limits batch size to 2 on SeaPerson due to memory constraints, yielding only 0.763 mAP@.5. Compressing features to 2 channels enables batch-8 training, boosting mAP@.5 by +1.5 pp to 0.778, reducing GFLOPs by 8.2\%, and further lifting Very Tiny recall by +1.2 pp.

\textbf{Error Bottleneck Diagnosis.} Coverage-loss supervision alone reduces selector false negatives from 25.6\% to 19.2\% on SeaPerson's semantic branch (BPR 0.947$\to$0.991), while dual-evidence routing adds more than 13,600 detections on UAVDT (total recall 85.17\%$\to$88.83\%), with BPR rising from 0.884 to 0.940, shifting the bottleneck to downstream regression.

\textbf{Latency Profiling.} On an RTX 5090 GPU, stem extraction takes 1.2 ms, selector 1.8 ms, AdaSlicer 0.9 ms, and ISPP 5.5 ms, achieving 106.1/80.6 FPS on UAVDT/SeaPerson respectively, demonstrating real-time throughput under this evaluation setup.
