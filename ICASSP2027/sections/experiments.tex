\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experiment Settings}
\label{ssec:settings}

We evaluate HESOD on two demanding high-resolution small-object benchmarks: \textbf{UAVDT}~\cite{UAVDT} ($1280 \times 1280\text{ px}$, urban vehicle surveillance across 3 classes) and \textbf{SeaPerson}~\cite{TinyPerson,SeaDronesSee} ($2048 \times 2048\text{ px}$, maritime search-and-rescue with $>85\%$ micro targets $<32\text{ px}$). Both evaluate full-resolution images without artificial cropping.

All models employ YOLOv5m in PyTorch and train for 50 epochs under selective protocols~\cite{ESOD}. Metrics include COCO $\mathrm{mAP@.5}$, Bounding Patch Recall (BPR), and scale-stratified recall across bins: \emph{Very Tiny} ($<16^2$), \emph{Tiny} ($16^2\text{--}32^2$), and \emph{Small} ($32^2\text{--}96^2$). Latency (FPS) is measured on a single NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU at batch size 1 with FP32 precision.

\textbf{Comparison with Dense Baselines.} As shown in Table~\ref{tab:main_results}, dense detectors incur prohibitive computational overhead on multi-megapixel grids. On \textbf{UAVDT} ($1280\times 1280$), HESOD outperforms Faster R-CNN and RetinaNet by \textbf{+5.8\,pp} and \textbf{+9.8\,pp} $\mathrm{mAP@.5}$ while slashing GFLOPs by \textbf{10.3$\times$} and \textbf{4.8$\times$} at \textbf{5.5$\times$} faster inference (\textbf{106.1}\,FPS vs.~18.8/19.8\,FPS), lifting total recall from 79.68\%/83.44\% to \textbf{88.83\%} (and Very Tiny recall from 70.35\%/72.77\% to \textbf{80.76\%}). On \textbf{SeaPerson} ($2048\times 2048$), the gains are even larger: HESOD boosts $\mathrm{mAP@.5}$ by \textbf{+22.2\,pp} and \textbf{+30.0\,pp} while cutting GFLOPs by \textbf{7.4$\times$} and \textbf{4.5$\times$} at \textbf{80.6}\,FPS. The recall gap is equally stark: dense detectors miss roughly a third of maritime instances (67.27\%/65.98\% vs.~\textbf{87.75\%}), particularly on Very Tiny targets (46.07\%/50.05\% vs.~\textbf{75.71\%}), confirming the necessity of dual-evidence selective computation.

\textbf{Comparison with Selective Baseline (ESOD).} Against ESOD, HESOD achieves substantial instance recall recovery and efficiency gains across both benchmarks (Table~\ref{tab:main_results}). The performance gains are particularly pronounced on the more challenging \textbf{SeaPerson} ($2048\times 2048$), where extreme scale disparity ($>85\%$ targets $<32$ px) causes severe baseline dropouts: HESOD boosts $\mathrm{mAP@.5}$ by \textbf{+2.3\,pp} ($0.750 \to \mathbf{0.773}$) and BPR by \textbf{+4.4\,pp} to a near-perfect \textbf{0.991} (total recall $+3.33$\,pp to \textbf{87.75\%}), while staged ISPP compression slashes parameters by \textbf{27.6\%} (25.92M vs. 35.78M) and sustains 80.6 FPS at 208.5 GFLOPs. On \textbf{UAVDT} ($1280\times 1280$), HESOD improves $\mathrm{mAP@.5}$ to \textbf{0.394} (+0.9\,pp) and BPR to \textbf{0.940} (+5.6\,pp, rescuing +13,700 targets), while staged training cuts parameters by \textbf{27.5\%} at 106.1 FPS (+3.66\,pp total recall). Recall improves across all aerial classes: \emph{car} ($85.6\% \to 89.0\%$), \emph{truck} ($81.1\% \to 86.5\%$), and \emph{bus} ($67.7\% \to 81.4\%$, \textbf{+13.7\,pp}).

\subsection{Ablation Studies and Scale Analysis}
\label{ssec:ablation_analysis}

\textbf{Evidence Preservation Principle.} As reported in Table~\ref{tab:ablation_and_recall}, unconstrained affine concatenation (Arm 5, 0.371 $\mathrm{mAP}$) degrades performance because unconstrained linear combinations dilute confident cues. In contrast, parameter-free Elementwise Max (Arm 6) enforces monotonic evidence preservation ($z_{i,c} \ge \max(z^{\mathrm{sem}}_{i,c}, z^{\mathrm{spec}}_{i,c})$), achieving peak selector accuracy of \textbf{0.395} $\mathrm{mAP}$ on UAVDT and \textbf{0.778} $\mathrm{mAP}$ on SeaPerson (0.991 BPR) while maintaining stable high-IoU localization ($\mathrm{mAP@.5:.95}$ of 0.218 and 0.330).

\textbf{Efficiency Recovery via Staged Training.} The inverted residual ISPP head serves as a dedicated compute compensator. On UAVDT, staged training freezes the converged selector before fine-tuning ISPP (Arm 7), preserving 0.394 $\mathrm{mAP@.5}$ and 0.940 BPR while slashing GFLOPs by 16.9\% (90.1 $\to$ 74.9) and parameters by \textbf{27.5\%} at 106.1 FPS. On SeaPerson, staged ISPP fine-tuning similarly cuts GFLOPs by \textbf{18.2\%} (255.1 $\to$ 208.5) and parameters by \textbf{27.6\%} (35.79M $\to$ 25.92M) with BPR fully preserved at 0.991, raising FPS from 73.8 to 80.6 (+9.2\%) with minimal accuracy trade-off (0.778 $\to$ 0.773 mAP).

\textbf{Micro-Scale Recall Recovery.} In the scale breakdown of Table~\ref{tab:ablation_and_recall}, baseline ESOD suffers severe degradation on \emph{Very Tiny} targets ($<16^2$ px), dropping to $79.43\%$ on UAVDT and $74.03\%$ on SeaPerson due to early semantic collapse. Dual-Max routing restores extreme-scale sensitivity across both domains, boosting \emph{Very Tiny} recall by \textbf{+6.26\,pp} on UAVDT and improving \emph{Tiny} recall by \textbf{+5.28\,pp} on SeaPerson ($92.06\%$ vs. $86.78\%$, with Very Tiny recall reaching \textbf{75.71\%}).

\textbf{Channel Pooling Benefit.} Comparing unpooled spectral filtering (Arm 3) with channel pooling (Arm 4) confirms that channel pooling maintains strictly $\mathcal{O}(1)$ width complexity while improving \emph{Very Tiny} recall across both benchmarks: \textbf{+3.10\,pp} on UAVDT ($83.78\% \to 86.88\%$) and \textbf{+0.90\,pp} on SeaPerson ($75.23\% \to 76.13\%$), effectively suppressing intra-channel noise and preventing VRAM spikes.

\textbf{Error Bottleneck Diagnosis.} Error analysis reveals that dual-evidence routing and coverage optimization slash selector false negatives from $25.6\%$ to \textbf{$19.2\%$} on UAVDT and achieve a near-flawless \textbf{99.1\%} BPR on SeaPerson, virtually eliminating early selector dropouts and shifting the remaining challenge to downstream regression refinement.

\textbf{Latency Profiling.} On an RTX 5090 GPU, stem extraction takes 1.2\,ms, Dual-Evidence selection takes 1.8\,ms, AdaSlicer takes 0.9\,ms, and ISPP execution takes 5.5\,ms, reaching 106.1\,FPS on UAVDT and 80.6\,FPS on SeaPerson.
