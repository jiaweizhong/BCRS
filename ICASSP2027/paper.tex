\begin{abstract}
Detecting tiny objects in high-resolution aerial and maritime imagery is severely challenged by background redundancy and extreme scale variation. While spatial selective computation mitigates arithmetic overhead by routing candidate foreground regions, existing single-evidence selectors suffer from severe semantic collapse on micro targets, causing irrecoverable coarse-stage pruning. We propose \textbf{HESOD} (\textbf{H}igh-Resolution \textbf{E}fficient \textbf{S}mall \textbf{O}bject \textbf{D}etection), a dual-evidence framework featuring semantic-spectral routing and coverage-constrained spatial optimization. HESOD couples high-level semantic objectness with channel-pooled spectral saliency at $\mathcal{O}(1)$ spatial complexity to rescue faint targets under an adaptive evidence preservation principle. To guarantee recall safety, an object-level soft-coverage loss directly optimizes multi-cell joint coverage probability across all ground-truth instances. Combined with an inverted residual decoupled head and scale-aware Wasserstein regression, HESOD advances $\mathrm{mAP@.5}$ by +0.7\,pp on UAVDT and +2.4\,pp on SeaPerson while cutting parameters by 27.6\% with negligible FLOPs overhead, establishing a superior accuracy-efficiency Pareto frontier for edge platforms.
\end{abstract}
