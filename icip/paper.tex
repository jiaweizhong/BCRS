\begin{abstract}
High-resolution aerial and maritime imagery presents severe computational challenges for small object detection due to pervasive background redundancy. While spatial selective-computation detectors dynamically route processing to candidate regions, existing selectors rely strictly on a single semantic objectness score. When micro targets undergo downsampling, their semantic activations frequently collapse, causing irrecoverable early pruning under asymmetric selection costs. In this paper, we propose \textbf{HESOD} (\textbf{H}igh-Resolution \textbf{E}fficient \textbf{S}mall \textbf{O}bject \textbf{D}etection), a framework based on semantic-spectral dual-evidence fusion and coverage-constrained spatial routing. HESOD introduces an ultra-lightweight priority head that fuses semantic objectness with channel-pooled spectral saliency at strictly $\mathcal{O}(1)$ complexity relative to channel width, effectively rescuing weak semantic targets. Furthermore, we formulate an object-level soft-coverage loss that directly maximizes joint instance coverage probability, fundamentally preventing premature false-negative drops. Paired with an inverted residual partial-convolution head and scale-aware Wasserstein regression, HESOD improves $\mathrm{mAP@.5}$ by +2.4\,pp on SeaPerson with 27.6\% fewer parameters while sustaining real-time throughput at 80.7 FPS.
\end{abstract}
