\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

High-resolution aerial and maritime vision is essential for remote surveillance, search-and-rescue, and environmental monitoring~\cite{VisDrone,UAVDT}. In these applications, targets of interest typically occupy merely a handful of pixels ($<16\times 16$ px), while uniform background dominates over $70\%$ to $80\%$ of the spatial area~\cite{ESOD,TinyPerson}. Executing dense deep detectors across multi-megapixel grids incurs prohibitive arithmetic and memory overhead on edge platforms.

To eliminate redundant computations, \emph{spatial selective computation} has emerged as an effective paradigm~\cite{AutoFocus,QueryDet,CEASC,ESOD}. Frameworks such as QueryDet~\cite{QueryDet} and CEASC~\cite{CEASC} sparsify feature pyramids, while ESOD~\cite{ESOD} predicts an early-stage spatial heatmap after the stem to route only candidate foreground patches to downstream stages.

Despite substantial compute savings, existing spatial selectors suffer from a critical vulnerability: they rely exclusively on a single semantic objectness score. When tiny targets undergo feature downsampling, their semantic activations frequently collapse into background noise~\cite{SET}. More crucially, spatial routing decisions exhibit an \emph{asymmetric cost}: retaining an extra background patch only marginally increases latency, whereas discarding a patch with a true tiny target creates an irrecoverable false negative for all subsequent stages~\cite{ESOD}. Furthermore, standard pixel-wise cross-entropy supervision evaluates grid cells independently, providing no guarantee that at least one candidate cell covers a given ground-truth instance.

To overcome these limitations, we observe that tiny targets preserve distinctive high-frequency structural gradients and spectral saliency even when high-level semantic activations fade~\cite{SET}. We propose \textbf{HESOD} (High-Resolution Efficient Small Object Detection), a framework integrating multi-evidence routing with coverage-constrained spatial optimization. Specifically, HESOD introduces an ultra-lightweight dual-evidence priority head that fuses semantic objectness with channel-pooled spectral saliency at strictly $\mathcal{O}(1)$ complexity relative to backbone width. To ensure recall safety, we design an object-level soft-coverage loss that directly maximizes the joint coverage probability of ground-truth instances. Finally, an inverted residual partial-convolution (ISPP) decoupled head and scale-aware balancing loss (SABL)~\cite{SSABNet} are integrated to accelerate inference and stabilize micro-scale box regression.

Our key contributions are summarized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We identify the semantic collapse failure mode under asymmetric selection costs and design a dual-evidence priority head fusing semantic and spectral saliency at negligible overhead.
    \item We formulate a differentiable object-level soft-coverage loss that optimizes joint instance coverage, fundamentally preventing premature false negatives on micro targets.
    \item We incorporate an inverted residual ISPP decoupled head with scale-aware Wasserstein regression, achieving state-of-the-art recall, accuracy, and throughput on UAVDT and SeaPerson.
\end{enumerate}
