Tables~\ref{tab:uavdt} and~\ref{tab:seaperson_main} evaluate HESOD against representative dense detectors (Faster R-CNN, RetinaNet) and the state-of-the-art selective detector (ESOD). While standard dense detectors execute uniform convolutions across entire multi-megapixel feature grids—incurring heavy compute overhead on edge devices—selective spatial detectors dynamically bypass background regions. 

Within the selective detection paradigm, HESOD sets a new accuracy and recall frontier across both aerial and maritime domains:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Urban Aerial Perception (UAVDT):} On $1280\times 1280$ images (Table~\ref{tab:uavdt}), HESOD achieves a \textbf{+2.7 pp} gain in $\mathrm{mAP@.5}$ (0.344 vs.\ 0.371), a \textbf{+2.4 pp} increase in $\mathrm{mAP@.5:.95}$ (0.171 vs.\ 0.195), and consistently improves per-class vehicle recall across \emph{car} ($84.87\% \to 90.56\%$, \textbf{+5.69 pp}), \emph{truck} ($84.41\% \to 84.88\%$), and \emph{bus} ($75.20\% \to 76.28\%$).
    \item \textbf{Maritime Search-and-Rescue (SeaPerson):} On ultra-high-resolution $2048\times 2048$ whole frames (Table~\ref{tab:seaperson_main}), HESOD elevates $\mathrm{mAP@.5}$ by \textbf{+1.9 pp} (0.750 $\to$ 0.769) with a 27.6\% parameter reduction (35.81M $\to$ 25.92M) while maintaining real-time throughput at 77.1 FPS, demonstrating superior efficiency-accuracy trade-offs.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Component-Wise Factorial Ablation.} Table~\ref{tab:seaperson_ablation} dissects the causal mechanisms across seven systematic configurations on SeaPerson ($2048\times 2048$):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Loss Function Impact (Arm 1 $\to$ 2):} Holding selector architecture strictly identical, replacing BCE with object-level soft-coverage loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cover}}$) delivers a sharp \textbf{+1.9 pp} surge in $\mathrm{mAP@.5}$ (0.750 $\to$ 0.769) and boosts Bounding Patch Recall (BPR) from 0.947 to \textbf{0.991}, confirming that multi-cell joint coverage optimization fundamentally prevents premature tiny-target pruning.
    \item \emph{Spectral Saliency as an Orthogonal Cue (Arm 2 vs.\ 3):} Remarkably, the spectral-only selector matches semantic-only accuracy (0.770 vs.\ 0.769 $\mathrm{mAP@.5}$, 0.992 BPR) despite utilizing zero semantic objectness signals. This empirical finding validates that high-frequency structural gradients extracted via channel pooling provide an orthogonal, dependable spatial routing cue at negligible $\mathcal{O}(1)$ compute cost.
    \item \emph{Evidence Fusion Dynamics (Arm 4 vs.\ 5):} Feature concatenation in Arm (4) achieves peak $\mathrm{mAP@.5}$ (\textbf{0.772}). Conversely, learned gating in Arm (5) yields lower accuracy (0.765 $\mathrm{mAP@.5}$) because the sigmoid gate tends to over-suppress routing area (Occupy drops to 0.330), proving unconditional dual-evidence concatenation is more recall-safe for tiny objects.
    \item \emph{Scale-Aware Box Regression (Arm 4 $\to$ 6):} Integrating SABL in Arm (6) achieves a balanced 0.771 $\mathrm{mAP@.5}$ while reducing Occupy (0.374 $\to$ 0.348) and complexity (281.2 $\to$ 263.7 GFLOPs, 74.8 FPS). Normalized Wasserstein regression provides continuous distance gradients for micro targets, accelerating box convergence and tightening spatial patch clustering.
    \item \emph{Lightweight Decoupled Head (Arm 6 $\to$ 7):} Swapping the standard detection head with the inverted residual ISPP decoupled head cleanly isolates head efficiency: parameter count drops by \textbf{27.6\%} (35.81M $\to$ 25.92M) and arithmetic complexity drops by \textbf{20.7\%} (263.7 $\to$ 209.1 GFLOPs), lifting inference frame rate to 77.1 FPS. Crucially, \emph{Very Tiny} recall surges to its global maximum of \textbf{77.19\%} (capturing 63,619 micro-targets, +2,605 over baseline).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Scale-Stratified Diagnostics and Error Shift.} Table~\ref{tab:size_buckets} reports matched detector recall across all 300,375 test instances in SeaPerson, partitioned into four physical area bins: \emph{Very Tiny} ($<16^2$ px, 82,417 targets), \emph{Tiny} ($16^2-32^2$ px, 174,816 targets), \emph{Small} ($32^2-96^2$ px, 42,987 targets), and \emph{Medium/Large} ($>96^2$ px, 155 targets).
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Dominant Tiny Target Recovery:} The largest absolute target recovery occurs in the dominant \emph{Tiny} stratum, where detector recall jumps by \textbf{+4.79 pp} (86.78\% $\to$ 91.57\% in Arm 2), successfully recovering over 8,380 previously dropped targets.
    \item \emph{Extreme-Scale Recovery via Dual Evidence and Full HESOD:} For the most challenging \emph{Very Tiny} instances ($<16^2$ px), dual-evidence concatenation (Arm 4) lifts recall to 76.00\% (+623 targets over semantic-only). Integrating SABL and the ISPP decoupled head in full HESOD (Arm 7) propels \emph{Very Tiny} recall to its global maximum of \textbf{77.19\%} (+2,605 targets over baseline, recovering 63,619 micro-targets), proving that dual-evidence routing paired with Wasserstein regression and lightweight decoupled heads provides critical resilience when semantic features collapse.
    \item \emph{Fundamental Error Bottleneck Shift:} Diagnostic tracking of missed tiny targets confirms that soft coverage and dual evidence reduce early selector-dropped error rate from $25.6\%$ in baseline ESOD down to \textbf{$19.2\%$} ($-6.4\text{ pp}$). This successfully cures early-stage spatial pruning failures, shifting the remaining detection challenge toward downstream localization refinement.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Discussion and Operating Regimes.} Our empirical findings delineate the operational boundaries of selective small-object detection:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Optimal Working Regime:} HESOD achieves maximum recall and computational advantages in \emph{ultra-high-resolution, spatially sparse environments} ($>80\%$ uniform background), such as maritime search-and-rescue and high-altitude surveillance. In this domain, dual-evidence priority estimation and soft-coverage optimization effectively prevent catastrophic false negatives while bypassing extensive background computation.
    \item \emph{Dense Scene Dynamics:} Conversely, in congested scenes with foreground targets uniformly distributed across every image tile (e.g., dense urban intersections in VisDrone \cite{VisDrone}), spatial skipping margins naturally narrow. In such environments, selective computation yields moderate throughput gains rather than order-of-magnitude compute reduction.
\end{itemize}
